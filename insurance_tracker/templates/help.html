1. Getting started

Signing in

Open your Broker Desk web address (for example https://yourname.pythonanywhere.com) and enter your password. You stay signed in on that device for two weeks. To sign out, click Sign out at the top right. Always sign out on a shared or public computer.

Bookmark the address on your phone too. Every page works on a phone.

The menu

Menu itemWhat it's for
DashboardToday at a glance: payments overdue and due, this week's meetings, to-dos and renewals.
ClientsEveryone you insure: contact details and all their policies.
PoliciesEvery policy, with filters by type and status.
RemindersWho needs to pay, who is renewing, and your own to-do list. The red number shows how many things are overdue.
CalendarYour meetings, plus payment dates, renewals and to-dos, month by month.
Monthly TrackerMonth by month: who renews, who paid, and whether commission came in.

The search box at the top finds clients by name, phone or email, and policies by policy number, insurer or insured item (such as a vehicle registration).

First-time setup (5 minutes)

  1. Go to Reports → Letterhead & business details and enter your business name, your name, address, phone and email. These print at the top of every report and letter.
  2. Go to Import and upload your latest renewal listing and commission statements (see section 9). This fills in most of your clients and policies for you.
  3. Add any clients and policies that aren't on those reports by hand (see section 4).

3. The Dashboard

{{ shot('dashboard', 'The Dashboard') }}
  • Top boxes: number of clients and active policies, total annual premium in force, estimated annual commission, commission actually received this year, and payments overdue (red when there are any).
  • Payments overdue: active policies whose payment date has passed. Each row shows the client's phone number, an Email reminder button that opens your email with a polite payment reminder already written, and Record payment.
  • Payments due in the next 14 days: the same, for payments coming up.
  • Next 7 days on the calendar: your meetings. Click one to open it.
  • To-do reminders: tick the box when something is done.
  • Renewals in the next 60 days: with Email client and Schedule review buttons.

4. Clients

Adding a client

  1. Click Clients → + New client (or + Client on the Dashboard).
  2. Fill in the name and whatever contact details you have. Only the last name is required.
  3. For a business (such as "Coral Bay Traders Limited"), leave First name blank and put the business name in Last name / business name.
  4. Click Save.

The client page

{{ shot('client', 'A client page: policies, meetings, contact details and reminders together.') }}

From a client's page you can add a policy or meeting for them, edit their details, and add a reminder that's linked to them. Click a phone number to call it from your phone, or an email address to write to them.

Deleting a client (Edit client → Delete) also deletes all their policies, payments and reminders. It can't be undone, so take a backup first if unsure.

Adding a policy

Open the client and click + Policy, or use Policies → + New policy.

6. Recording payments

{{ shot('policy', 'A policy page: details on the left, Record a payment on the right.') }}
  1. Open the policy (from the client page, the Policies list, or Record payment on the Dashboard).
  2. Under Record a payment, check the date, amount and method. Add a cheque or receipt number in Notes if you like.
  3. Leave Move next due date forward ticked. The next payment date then moves on by one month, quarter, half-year or year, according to the payment frequency.
  4. Click Save payment.

If the payment is the renewal premium, the policy also moves into its new one-year term automatically, and it drops off the renewals list.

Every payment is listed under Payment history. If you made a mistake, click Remove next to the payment. The due date isn't changed back automatically, so correct it with Edit policy if needed.

The Email payment reminder button at the top of a policy opens your email program with a reminder to the client already written. Read it, change it if you like, and send.

7. Reminders

{{ shot('reminders', 'The Reminders page') }}
  • Overdue payments: clients who still owe. Highlighted in red.
  • Payments due in the next… days: choose 7, 14, 30, 60 or 90 days at the top.
  • Renewals in the next 60 days.
  • My to-do reminders: type what you need to remember, pick a date and (optionally) a client, then click Add reminder. Tick the box when it's done.

When an imported renewal listing says a policy has an outstanding premium, a to-do reminder "Collect outstanding premium – policy …" is created for you automatically.

Reminders show inside Broker Desk. They don't send you texts or emails, so make opening the Dashboard part of your morning.

8. Calendar and meetings

{{ shot('calendar', 'The Calendar: meetings (blue), payments due (red), renewals (orange) and to-dos (purple).') }}
  • Click a day number to add a meeting on that day, or click + New meeting.
  • Give it a title, type (client meeting, renewal review, new quote, claim help, insurer visit and so on), client (optional), time, place and notes. Click Save.
  • Click a meeting to change or delete it.
  • Add to phone / Outlook (.ics) on a meeting downloads it as a calendar entry with a 30-minute alert. Open the file on your phone or computer to add it to your own calendar.
  • Upcoming list shows all future meetings in one list.

9. Importing insurer spreadsheets

Broker Desk reads the Excel reports the insurance company sends you, so you don't have to type them in.

ReportWhat the import does
Renewal listing
LOB, POLICY, RENEWAL DATE, CLIENT NAME…		 Adds or updates each client (address, phone) and policy (cover, sum insured, vehicle or property, discounts, premium). It sets the renewal date and the payment due, so they appear in Reminders, the Calendar and the Monthly Tracker. Policies with an outstanding premium get a reminder.
Commission statement
ProdCode, InvPrefix, PolicyNo, InvoiceNo, Comm%, CommAmt…		 Records every commission line on the Commissions page. It fills in the policy's period of insurance and commission rate, and marks those renewals as paid.

How to import

  1. Click Import.
  2. Click Choose files and select one or more spreadsheets (.xls, .xlsx or .csv). You can select several at once.
  3. Check the Insurance company name. It's given to any new policies from these files.
  4. Click Import and read the results list.
{{ shot('import-results', 'Import results: every client and policy added or updated, each with a link.') }}
  • Importing the same file twice is safe. Clients are matched by name, policies by policy number (even when written differently, such as "PCP 587 368 000" and 587368) and commission lines by invoice number. Nothing is doubled.
  • If a file can't be read, you'll see a red line "… was NOT imported. Nothing from this file was saved." with the reason. Nothing from that file is saved, so you can fix the file and try again.
  • Where the insurer's class code had to be interpreted (for example PCL = Private Car Comprehensive), the policy's notes say so. Correct the policy type with Edit policy if needed.

10. The Monthly Tracker

The Monthly Tracker answers three questions for every month: who renews, who paid, and did the commission come in?

{{ shot('tracker', 'The Monthly Tracker: one row per policy, one column per month.') }}
  • Each coloured box is a renewal, showing the date, premium, amount paid and commission (with the statement it was on).
  • Month totals at the top: renewals, paid, not paid, premium still to collect, commission received, commission still expected, and that month's statement total.
  • Use Earlier / This month / Later and the months box to change the period.
  • Use the filter to show only one group, for example:
    • Not paid (past due) gives a list of people to chase.
    • Paid, commission not received gives a list of commissions to query with the insurer.
  • Download Excel saves the same grid, in colour, with a month-totals sheet.

A renewal counts as paid if you recorded a payment for it or the insurer paid commission on it, because insurers only pay commission once the premium is collected.

11. Commissions

{{ shot('commissions', 'The Commissions page') }}

Shows every commission line imported from statements: client, policy, invoice, term, premium, rate and commission. The boxes at the top give each statement month's total. 12. Reports and printing

{{ shot('reports', 'The Reports page') }}

For clients

  • Client policy summary: a letter to the client listing all their policies, what's coming due, and payments received.
  • Renewal notice: a renewal letter for one policy with the renewal date, premium and cover details.

Client letters never show your commission.

For insurance companies and queries

  • Commission reconciliation / query: commission received in the months you choose, plus three query lists: renewals paid with no commission yet, adjustment lines to confirm, and renewals not yet paid.
  • Renewals for a period: everyone renewing in the chosen months, with status.
  • Outstanding premiums: unpaid renewals in the last 12 months, plus premiums the insurer flagged.
  • Book of business: every policy with period, sum insured, annual premium and estimated commission.
  • Policy history: one policy in full, for answering an insurer's query.
{{ shot('report-commission', 'A report ready to print, on your letterhead.') }}

Printing or saving as PDF

  1. Choose the report and options, then click Open report.
  2. Click Print / Save as PDF.
  3. In the print window, choose your printer, or choose Save as PDF as the destination to email the report. The menu and buttons aren't printed.

Insurer reports also have Download Excel.

13. Backups

Everything is stored in one database file on your website. To keep a copy:

  1. Go to Import.
  2. Click Download backup. A file named like insurance-backup-2026-11-03.db is saved.
  3. Keep it somewhere safe: email it to yourself, or copy it to a USB drive or cloud folder.

Do this at least once a month (after your monthly import) and before deleting anything. The backup contains clients' personal information, so keep it private.

Restoring a backup

Go to Import → Restore from a backup, choose the backup file, tick the box and click Restore this backup. All the current data is replaced by the backup. Before that happens, a safety copy of the current data is saved in the backups folder. Your sign-in password stays the same.

14. Running it on your own computer or office network

Broker Desk can also run on your own laptop or office PC, with nothing stored on the internet. You use it in the browser exactly as now, and other computers and phones on the same office network or Wi-Fi can sign in too. The READ ME FIRST.txt file in the Broker Desk folder has the full steps. In short:

  1. Install Python from python.org. On Windows, tick Add python.exe to PATH.
  2. Unzip the BrokerDesk package into your Documents folder.
  3. Double-click Start Broker Desk (.bat on Windows, .command on Mac). The first start takes a minute or two. After that it starts in seconds.
  4. Your browser opens. Create your password.
  5. To bring your data across: on the online version, click Import → Download backup; on the new copy, use Import → Restore from a backup.

Using it from other devices

The Broker Desk window shows an address like http://192.168.1.20:8080. Open it in the browser on any device on the same network and sign in. The main computer must be switched on, with the Broker Desk window open. On Windows, allow Python on Private networks when asked.

Where your data is

  • data/insurance.db: your database.
  • data/backups/: a backup is saved automatically each day (the last 60 are kept).
  • data/config.json: settings. "network": false allows only this computer. "port" changes the address number.

Copy the data folder to a USB drive or cloud folder now and then. To install a newer version, unzip it and copy your data folder into it.

Starting Broker Desk automatically on Windows

Do this once. Broker Desk then starts by itself each time you sign in to Windows.

  1. Open the Startup folder. Press the Windows key + R. In the small Run box, type shell:startup and press Enter. A folder window opens. Leave it open.
  2. Put a shortcut to Broker Desk in it. In another window, open your BrokerDesk folder. Right-click Start Broker Desk.bat and choose Show more options → Create shortcut (on Windows 10, just Create shortcut). Drag the new shortcut into the Startup folder.
  3. Optional: start it minimised. In the Startup folder, right-click the shortcut and choose Properties. Next to Run, choose Minimized, then click OK.
  4. Test it. Restart the computer and sign in. After a few seconds Broker Desk starts and your browser opens on it.
  • Don't want the browser to open each time? Open data\config.json in Notepad and change "open_browser": true to "open_browser": false.
  • Don't move or rename the BrokerDesk folder afterwards, or the shortcut stops working. If you do move it, delete the old shortcut and repeat step 2.
  • It starts when you sign in to Windows, not before. For other devices to use it, this computer must be on and signed in. Closing the Broker Desk window stops it.
  • To stop it starting automatically, delete the shortcut from the Startup folder.
  • If this is the office's main computer, set it never to sleep while plugged in (Settings → System → Power). Otherwise other devices lose their connection while it sleeps.

On a Mac: System Settings → General → Login Items → +, then choose Start Broker Desk.command.

Changing or resetting the password

Change it under Reports → Letterhead & business details → Change password. If you forget it: close Broker Desk, create an empty file named reset-password.txt in the data folder, start Broker Desk again, and create a new password on the main computer. Your data isn't affected.

15. Keeping the online website running

Every 3 months (free PythonAnywhere plan)

PythonAnywhere emails you before your site is paused. Log in at pythonanywhere.com, open the Web tab and click Run until 3 months from today. If you miss it, the site pauses but nothing is lost. Click the same button to start it again.

Getting new features and fixes

  1. On PythonAnywhere, open a Bash console (Dashboard → $ Bash).
  2. Type bash ~/OP_Apps/deploy/update.sh and press Enter.
  3. When it says Updated, go to the Web tab and press the green Reload button.

Updates never touch your data.

Changing your password

On the Web tab, open the WSGI configuration file, change the text in the TRACKER_PASSWORD line, click Save, then Reload. Everyone is signed out and must use the new password.

16. Troubleshooting and questions

The site says "Something went wrong"
The page shows a short error message. Nothing from that action was saved. Send the message to whoever looks after the app. For the full details, on PythonAnywhere open the Web tab, then Error log, and copy the last lines.
An import says "NOT imported"
Nothing from that file was saved. The message gives the reason. The most common cause is a report in a different layout. Send the file and the message to whoever looks after the app.
A client appears twice
Names are matched exactly (ignoring capitals and punctuation), so "Stephen" and "Stephanie" are different clients. If the same person was typed two ways, move their policies to one client (Edit policy → Client) and delete the empty one.
A renewal shows as "Not paid" but the client paid
Open the policy and Record payment. When the commission statement arrives and is imported, the box turns green.
The dates look one day off
Broker Desk uses Trinidad and Tobago time. If you work in another time zone, ask for TRACKER_TZ to be changed in the WSGI file.
I forgot my password
Online version: set a new one in the WSGI file (see section 15). Own computer: see section 14.
Can more than one person use it?
Yes. Anyone with the web address and password can sign in, and they all see the same data.
